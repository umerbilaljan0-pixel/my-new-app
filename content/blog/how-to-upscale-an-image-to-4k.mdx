"Make it 4K" is easy to ask for and easy to get wrong. The trick is matching the
target to what your source can actually support.

## Targets, not multipliers

CLEANPLATE's [Upscale tool](/upscale-image) asks for an output **resolution** —
1080p, 2K or 4K — not a vague "×4". You shouldn't have to know your source's pixel
count to make a good choice. Pick the size you need; the tool works out the scale,
runs it, and resamples to the exact dimensions while preserving your aspect ratio.

## The 4× rule

A target is greyed out when it would enlarge the source more than 4×. Past that,
you're inventing far more pixels than you started with, and it shows. For a small
image, **2K is often the honest best result** — the note under the greyed-out 4K
option tells you so.

## What upscaling can and can't do

- **Can:** sharpen soft detail, smooth jagged edges, make a small image usable at
  a larger size.
- **Can't:** recover detail that was never captured. If text is an unreadable
  smudge in the source, upscaling makes a *sharper* smudge, not readable text.

## Don't over-sharpen

Sharpening is off by default, on purpose — cranked sharpening looks cheap and
crunchy. Add a little if an image is soft; leave it at zero if it's already crisp.

## Chain it

Removed a background and now need it bigger? Send the cut-out straight into
Upscale — no re-upload. The result of one tool becomes the input to the next.
