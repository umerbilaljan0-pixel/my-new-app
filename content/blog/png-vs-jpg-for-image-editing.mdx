Most "why does my image look bad?" problems come down to the wrong format at the
wrong step. Here's the short version.

## Use PNG when you need transparency

If any part of your image is see-through — a cut-out product, a logo, an overlay —
you need **PNG**. It's the only common web format that stores a real alpha
channel. Export a background-removed image as JPG and the transparency collapses
to a solid colour (usually white).

## Use JPG for photos without transparency

For a full-frame photo with no transparency, **JPG** is smaller at the same
perceived quality. A 4000px photo as PNG can be several times larger than the same
photo as a high-quality JPG, with no visible benefit.

## Don't re-save JPGs over and over

JPG is *lossy*: every save throws away a little detail. Edit from the best copy you
have, and avoid a chain of "open, tweak, re-save as JPG" — the artefacts compound.
When you're doing several edits, keep an intermediate PNG and export to JPG once at
the end.
