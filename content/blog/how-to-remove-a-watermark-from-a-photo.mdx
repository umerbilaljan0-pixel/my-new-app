Watermarks are meant to be hard to remove. But when you own the image — or you've
licensed it and just need a clean copy — you shouldn't have to fight your tools.
Here's the fast way to do it well.

## Before you start: make sure you're allowed

Removing an ownership mark, credit line or copyright notice from someone else's
work is unlawful in most countries, regardless of the tool. This guide is for
images **you own or are licensed to modify**. If that's not you, stop here.

## Step 1 — Drop the image in

Open the [Erase tool](/remove-watermark) and drop your file. Everything is
processed in your browser first, then uploaded over a secure link. Your image is
deleted from our servers within 24 hours, and its EXIF metadata is stripped
before it ever leaves your device.

## Step 2 — Let detection do the first pass

On upload, CLEANPLATE looks for semi-transparent overlays and repeated marks and
draws them as cyan boxes. If it found your watermark, click **Erase detected
areas**. If it missed, or grabbed something you want to keep, choose **Draw it
myself** instead — nothing is erased until you confirm.

## Step 3 — Paint slightly past the edges

This is the single biggest factor in a clean result. Brush over the watermark and
extend a few pixels past its edge. The mask is expanded slightly before
processing, so painting right up to the boundary tends to leave a faint halo,
while going a touch wider does not.

## Step 4 — Download

Grab the free 1200px version for web and social, or full resolution with a
credit. Either way, only the pixels you masked were rebuilt — the rest of the
image is identical to your original.

## A note on quality

Erase rebuilds what was *probably* behind the mark. It's excellent over smooth or
regular areas (a sky, a wall, a gradient) and good over texture. Over a busy,
detailed subject, expect a plausible fill rather than a perfect reconstruction —
and keep your masks tight.
